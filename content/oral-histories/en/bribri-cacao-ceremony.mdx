# The Sacred Cacao of the Bribri

<Callout type="info" title="About This Story">
  This oral history represents traditional Bribri knowledge about the cacao tree
  (_Theobroma cacao_), known as **Tsirú** in the Bribri language. The narrative
  draws on published ethnobotanical research and community-shared knowledge.
</Callout>

## The Gift of Sibö̀

In Bribri cosmology, cacao was not simply discovered — it was transformed by
**Sibö̀**, the creator god. According to tradition, Sibö̀ turned his own niece,
**Tsirú**, into the cacao tree so that her sweetness and nourishment would
sustain the Bribri people forever. This is why cacao is considered sacred — it
carries the spirit of a loved one within its pods.

The elders say that every time we prepare the ceremonial cacao drink, we are
honoring this gift. The chocolate is not just food; it is a connection to our
ancestors and to Sibö̀ himself.

## Preparing the Ceremonial Drink

The traditional preparation of Bribri cacao is specific and meaningful:

1. The ripe pods are harvested carefully — never pulled, but cut with a clean
   blade
2. The beans are fermented for several days in banana leaves
3. They are dried slowly in the sun, turned by hand
4. Roasted over a wood fire until the shells crack
5. Ground on a stone _metate_ by the women of the clan
6. Mixed with warm water and sometimes sweetened with _dulce de tapa_ (raw cane
   sugar)

The drink is served in **jícaras** — the dried gourd vessels made from the
jícaro tree. The elders say cacao tastes different when drunk from a jícara than
from any other vessel.

## Cacao in Ceremonies

Cacao appears in the most important moments of Bribri life:

- **Funerals**: The chocolate drink is prepared and shared among mourners. It
  is said to ease the spirit's journey
- **Birth celebrations**: New mothers receive cacao to restore their strength
- **House building**: When a new traditional _ù-suré_ (conical house) is
  raised, cacao is shared with all who helped
- **Healing ceremonies**: The _awá_ (traditional healer) may prescribe cacao as
  part of treatment for certain ailments

## The Cacao Garden

In the Bribri way, cacao is never planted alone. It grows beneath the shade of
taller trees — banana plants for quick shade, and great trees like the
**pejibaye** and forest giants for permanent canopy. This is not just
agriculture; it is how the forest works.

> "The cacao tree teaches us that nothing in the forest lives alone. It needs
> its companions — the shade above, the roots beside it, the insects that visit
> its flowers. When we plant cacao the old way, we are planting a small forest."

## A Living Tradition

Today, Bribri communities in Talamanca continue to grow cacao using traditional
methods. Some families maintain cacao gardens that have been tended for
generations, with trees that are decades old. The knowledge of which trees
produce the best chocolate, when to harvest, and how to prepare the ceremonial
drink passes from grandmother to granddaughter.

Modern chocolate-making has come to Talamanca too, but the ceremonial use
remains distinct. The cacao prepared for ceremony follows the old ways — it is
a different thing entirely from the chocolate bars sold in San José.
