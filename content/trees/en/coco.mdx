allergenDetails: "Coconut allergy is uncommon (estimated 0.5% of population) and is often less severe than other tree nut allergies. Many people with tree nut allergies can safely consume coconut. Coconut is botanically a drupe, not a true nut, but regulatory agencies often classify it as a tree nut for labeling purposes."
structuralRiskDetails: "PRIMARY HAZARD: FALLING COCONUTS. Mature coconuts weigh 1-4 kg (2-9 lbs) and fall from heights of 20-30 meters. Falling coconuts cause serious injuries and occasional fatalities - estimated 150 deaths per year worldwide. Coconuts can fall at any time but risk increases during storms and high winds. Fronds (leaves) also fall and can cause injury - each frond weighs 5-25 kg. NEVER park vehicles or place seating directly under coconut palms. In public areas, regular harvesting of mature coconuts is essential. Coastal resorts often remove coconuts when green to prevent injury."
safetyNotes: "FOOD IS SAFE - FALLING COCONUTS ARE THE MAIN DANGER. When visiting beaches with coconut palms: Do not sit, park, or camp directly beneath palms. Be aware during windy conditions. Watch for falling coconuts (you may hear them fall nearby first). In landscaped areas, coconuts should be harvested regularly before they mature and fall. If you must walk under coconut palms, do so quickly and stay alert. The famous warning 'Don't sleep under coconut trees' is real advice. Despite the falling hazard, coconut palms are valuable landscape trees when properly managed. Regular professional maintenance (removing coconuts and dead fronds) makes them safe for public areas."

<AccordionItem title="🔬 Taxonomic Notes">

_Cocos_ is a monotypic genus—meaning the coconut palm is the only species in its genus. The species epithet _nucifera_ means "nut-bearing."

**Etymology of "Coco"**: Portuguese explorers named it "coco" from the word for "grinning face" or "skull" because the three indentations on the coconut shell resemble a face.

**Varieties**: Two main forms exist:

- **Tall variety** — Traditional, 50-60 year lifespan, 15-20 m tall
- **Dwarf variety** — Smaller, earlier fruiting, shorter-lived

</AccordionItem>
