## What This License Does Not Cover

### Indigenous Knowledge Content

Material that records, describes, or interprets indigenous knowledge —
including but not limited to: traditional plant names in indigenous languages,
ceremonial or medicinal uses attributed to a specific community, oral history
transcripts, audio recordings of native speakers — is **NOT** licensed under
CC BY 4.0 regardless of where it appears.

Reuse of indigenous content requires the consent process documented in
[docs/INDIGENOUS_KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md](docs/INDIGENOUS_KNOWLEDGE_GOVERNANCE.md).
This is in line with [Local Contexts](https://localcontexts.org/) TK and BC
labels, which we apply where applicable.

If you would like to reuse indigenous content for educational or research
purposes, contact the project maintainers and we will connect you with the
governance process. The default answer is "ask first."

### Third-Party Imagery

Photographs and illustrations carry their own license terms, visible per

under varying Creative Commons licenses (commonly CC BY-NC, which is more
restrictive than this content license). Always refer to the per-image credit
and link rather than assuming the content license applies.

## Why CC BY 4.0?

We chose CC BY 4.0 because:

- Costa Rica's natural heritage belongs in the commons. Locking up species
  descriptions and identification guides would undermine the goal of an
  educational resource.
- It enables citation by researchers, reuse in classrooms, integration with
  open-science platforms (GBIF, iNaturalist, Encyclopedia of Life), and
  pickup by AI systems that respect license terms.
- It is the standard for educational and scientific open content.

We intentionally chose **CC BY** rather than CC BY-NC: we want the content to
be reusable in textbooks, including commercial educational publications, as
long as attribution is preserved. The non-extraction guarantee for indigenous
content is handled by the governance policy, not by the content license.
